# Alternative text for figures

Votes Without Neighborhoods: Housing Prices and the Geography of Conservative Voting in Seoul. Image descriptions for accessibility (WCAG 2.1 AA); one per figure, in the order of the manuscript.

**Figure 1.** Flow diagram. A box for the registered residents of one neighborhood, dong i, on the left has arrows to five voting modes in the middle: election day in the own voting district; in-zone early voting at the own dong's station; in-zone early voting at the station of another dong in the same zone; out-of-zone early voting by return envelope; and at-home, shipboard and overseas voting. Each mode has an arrow to where its ballots are reported, on the right: the first two are reported for dong i and attributed to its residents (green); the third is reported for the other dong j and misattributed (red); the last two are pooled at the district level, or the constituency level in 2024 (orange).

**Figure 2.** Three side-by-side panels sharing a vertical axis that lists the six elections from 2012 at the top to 2026 at the bottom; 2012 and 2017 are marked with the assumption columns used for them. Panel A, across districts: for all of Seoul, a dot with a short horizontal line shows a positive slope in every election, and a second marker for the districts without Gangnam, Seocho and Songpa lies closer to zero, especially in 2012 and 2017. Bars for 2012 and 2017, which rest on different assumptions, are drawn faint. Panel B, within-district slope: horizontal bars show the range of slopes consistent with the data under the main assumptions, for all of Seoul and without the three districts; only the bars for 2012 (all of Seoul), 2024 (all of Seoul) and 2026 lie entirely to the right of the zero line. Panel C, within-district contrast in percentage points: only the 2026 bar for all of Seoul lies entirely right of zero; all other bars cross zero.

**Figure 3.** Three maps of Seoul's neighborhoods for the 2026 mayoral election, with district boundaries outlined. Map (a) shades each neighborhood by Oh Se-hoon's conventional vote share; shares are highest in Gangnam, Seocho and Songpa and along the Han River, and lower in the north and west. Map (b) shades each neighborhood by the width of the range of its residents' share consistent with the returns alone; most neighborhoods have wide ranges, often 30 points or more. Map (c) uses the same scale for the range under mobility-calibrated movement with out-of-zone voters within ten points of their station; the ranges are much narrower almost everywhere.

**Figure 4.** Four heat-map panels, one per election (2022, 2024, 2025 and 2026). Rows are amounts of voter movement, from half the mobility-calibrated amount to unrestricted; columns are tolerances for out-of-zone voters from 0.1 to 1. Each cell shows the largest additional departure s* at which the within-district slope stays positive. The 2022 and 2025 panels are entirely gray: no positive lower bound anywhere. The 2024 panel has small values, mostly in the first column, and is gray for unrestricted movement. The 2026 panel shows infinity or large values in the first column, smaller values at tolerances of 0.15 and 0.2, and gray from 0.3 onward; the main setting, outlined, shows infinity.
